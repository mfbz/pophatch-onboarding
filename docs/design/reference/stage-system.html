Stage System

The visual rules behind the product screens, shown as rendered examples. Every value here is the value the prototype uses. The same rules, in words, are on the Notion page "PopHatch: Design". "The stage" is the top part of every page: the blue background with a patch of light and two white circles in the top-right corner. A "hairline" is a 1px white line at 26%. A "pill" is a shape with fully rounded ends. The column of page names on the left is the left bar, and the three cards on the right of Home and the pages are the right side, as on the product page.

Principles

Every page starts with the stage. Fans, Shows, Money, In progress, Conversations and Done also show one large number in lime on it. Home, a Plan, a conversation, Sources, Settings, Billing and Support have a shorter stage with the title and no number. The patch of light is always in the top-right corner, away from the text.
Lime is used only for these things. The large number on the stage and the second half of its label, the dot beside the first decision card, primary buttons, the active item in the left bar and the active tab on a phone, the IN PROGRESS heading in the left bar while the artist is on that page, the first bar of a bar chart and the main line of a line chart, the current step in a Plan, the artist's own messages in a conversation, the circle with the artist's initial in the account button, the word Open or Close on the "Where we are" strip on a phone, the dot of a working connection on Sources and the line around a row the artist has picked there, a checked box, and the fill of a progress bar. Nothing else is lime.
Sections, not boxes. Sections are separated by a hairline above each one, with no border on the sides and no background of their own. The only shapes with a background are the message bubbles in a conversation, the "Why" panel at the top of a Plan, and the writing box (the box where the artist writes to PopHatch).
Decision cards are numbered. 01, 02, 03. The first card's number is filled lime and has a lime dot beside it that grows and shrinks every 2.6 seconds; the other cards' numbers are white outlines.
Pink is a dot, never text. Pink text on Klein has a contrast of 3.4 to 1, below the 4.5 to 1 that small text needs. Every other text color here is above 6.9 to 1.
No icons. Everything is a word. The only drawn marks are the dots, the check mark (✓), the chevron (›) on a phone list row, and the arrow (←) on a phone top bar.

Buttons and the left bar

Every button is a pill (border radius 999px).

Primary: lime background, navy text, weight 700, 14px, padding 10px top and bottom and 18px left and right. On a decision card it names the first step of the Plan, as a verb and at most three words, and it is different on every card; it is never "Approve", "Start", "Open" or "Let's do it". Secondary: no background, white text, a 1.5px white border at 70%. Link: no background, Periwinkle text, underlined. When the mouse is over a primary button its lime goes to 90%; over a secondary button the border goes to white at 100%. While any button is pressed it shrinks to 96% of its size. Nothing else changes on hover or press.

HomeFansShowsMoneySources

Items in the left bar are pills, 15px, weight 600. The active item has a lime background and navy text; when the mouse is over an item, its background is white at 8%. The left bar is Night, 236px wide, with a 1px white line at 10% on its right edge. From top to bottom it holds Home, Fans, Shows, Money, then IN PROGRESS with its three Plans, CONVERSATIONS with its three conversations, and the account button at the bottom. A pink dot appears next to Sources when a connection needs the artist, and on "More" on a phone.

PopHatch's message bubbles are glass (white at 7%) with a 1px border at 13%, radius 22, and 15.5–16px text. The artist's bubbles are lime with navy text. The writing box is a pill with a white background at 8% and a 1.5px white border at 40%. Its placeholder text reads "Write to PopHatch" in a Plan or a conversation, and "What's on your mind?" on Home. On a phone the writing box is fixed at the bottom of the screen, above the tab bar.

Charts

Charts have no border and no background.

The Earl, Atlanta210
Aisle 5, Atlanta140
Smith's, Atlanta95

Each chart block is a section with a hairline above it, a title at 20–22px, one line in Periwinkle saying what the chart shows, and then the chart. In a bar chart the first bar is lime and the other bars are white at 85%; bars are 12px tall with fully rounded ends; the value at the end of each bar is Familjen Grotesk 15px 700. In a line chart the line for the thing the chart is about is lime, and a comparison line is white at 35%. On a computer the blocks sit on a grid of six columns: most blocks take three columns (two blocks per row), a wide block takes all six, and a narrow block takes two; the gap is 44px between columns and 18px between rows. On a phone there is one column, and each bar's label sits on its own line above the bar.

Three layouts

By the width of the window.

1300px and widerA computer. The left bar, the main column in the middle (at most 1180px, centered when the window is wider), and the right side. The right side stays in view while the main column scrolls, 20px from the top.
860px to 1299pxA tablet held sideways, a small laptop. The left bar stays. The right side moves under the main column, with a white line at 18% above it, its three cards side by side. On a Plan, "Where we are" moves under the conversation the same way. From 860px to 1099px the decision card uses the phone arrangement and the right side's three cards stack.
Narrower than 860pxA phone. One column and the tab bar at the bottom.

The account button at the bottom of the left bar opens a small menu above it with Sources, Settings, Billing and Support; the pink dot shows on Sources there too.

Text that is too long

Nothing is cut with three dots, with one exception: the title in the top bar on a phone stops after two lines. Everything else wraps. There are no tooltips; everything the artist needs to know is written on the screen.
